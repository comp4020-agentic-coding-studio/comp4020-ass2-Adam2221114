> Find a real apology this course hasn't already used, and work out what's
> actually wrong with it.

Weeks 2 through 5 gave you a way to take an apology apart: the component
model, the ablation test for which part is doing the work, the
non-apology's failure patterns, and the question of whose interests an
apology actually serves. This assessment is where that toolkit gets used
on something none of you have seen dissected before.

## The brief

Choose a real public apology — a company statement, a public figure's
statement, a brand's social media post, an official response to a
complaint. It has to be one this course hasn't examined, and specific
enough that you can quote it directly.

Diagnose it. Go component by component: is acknowledgment of harm present,
and is it specific or generic? Is responsibility accepted directly, or
softened, redirected, or missing? Is there a remedy commitment, and does
it commit to anything checkable? Then apply the ablation test from Week 3:
which component, if you removed it, would break this particular apology —
and is that the same component every apology in this course has depended
on, or something specific to this case?

Once you've located what's failing, work out why. Does it follow one of
Week 4's non-apology patterns — a conditional, a passive construction, a
redirection onto the recipient's feelings, responsibility withdrawn by an
excuse clause? Or is the wording adequate but built to serve the speaker's
reputation or need for closure more than the person actually harmed, per
Week 5? Name which, and show your evidence rather than asserting a
verdict.

Finally, redesign it. Produce a version of the apology that addresses
what your diagnosis found, and a short note — a few sentences, not a full
essay — connecting each change back to the specific failure it repairs. A
redesign that fixes problems you didn't diagnose, or leaves diagnosed
problems untouched, doesn't count.

## What you submit

The original apology (quoted or linked), your component-by-component
diagnosis, your non-apology/self-serving-motive analysis, and your
redesigned version with its change notes. There's no fixed length beyond
what the diagnosis and redesign genuinely require — padding the diagnosis
to look thorough is not the same as being thorough.
