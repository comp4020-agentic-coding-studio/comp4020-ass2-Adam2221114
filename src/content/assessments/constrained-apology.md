> Something has gone wrong. There isn't a clean way to respond to it. Decide
> what to do anyway, and be able to defend the decision.

The Apology Autopsy tested whether you could diagnose and rebuild an
apology that already existed. This assessment asks something harder: given
a situation, decide whether an apology belongs in the response at all, and
if so, who it's for, when it happens, and through what channel — using
Weeks 6 through 11 as your working material, not a checklist to tick off.

## Choose one scenario

**The delayed disclosure.** A senior clinician's decision led to a
documented complication. Two days after the incident became public
knowledge through local news coverage, the hospital's own investigation
isn't finished, and legal counsel isn't yet sure what can be said without
prejudicing it. The patient's family is asking, publicly, why no one has
said anything.

**The silence that was actually knowledge.** Three months ago, someone's
private detail was shared at a party. The person harmed has spent those
three months quietly suspecting the wrong friend. The person who actually
did it has known this the entire time and said nothing — not to protect
an investigation, just to avoid the conversation.

**The screenshot that outlived the post.** A company's social media
account briefly posted an internal joke referencing a competitor's
product recall. It was deleted within minutes, but not before it was
screenshotted and is now circulating. The CEO is being pushed, within
hours, for a same-day public response — before anyone internally has
established whether this was an authorised post or one employee's error.

## What your justification has to do

For your chosen scenario, decide which of apologise now, acknowledge harm
without a full apology, investigate first, act first, or delay actually
fits — and argue against at least two of the others by name, using what
they would cost in this specific situation. If you choose to delay,
apply Week 11's test directly: is the time you're proposing going to
produce genuine new understanding, or does it just let the responsible
party wait out the pressure while the harmed party is left without
acknowledgment? If timing or medium is part of your answer, use Week 7's
variables, not intuition. If power is part of what makes this scenario
hard, use Week 8 and 9 to say exactly whose position is doing the work.

## What you submit

Your justification, and the response itself, written out in the form it
would actually be delivered — not a description of what you'd say, but
the thing you'd actually send, publish, or speak. A justification that
argues for a text message and then produces a press release doesn't
count as coherent.
