---
title: The Ablation Test
description:
  Remove one component at a time from a working apology and identify which
  removal actually breaks it
number: 2
week: 3
weight: 1
spec:
  - you bring the apology you marked up in Lab 1 (or a new one, marked up
    the same way)
  - you can show which component's removal breaks your apology, and which
    removal barely changes it
  - you can explain why a component that broke your apology might not
    break someone else's
related:
  - lectures/week-03
---

Before the lab, have your Lab 1 marked-up apology ready — the one with
each component underlined and labelled present, weak, or missing. If
you're using a new example, mark it up the same way first; the ablation
exercise doesn't work on an un-annotated apology.

In the lab, working from that marked-up text, remove one component at a
time — cross it out, don't just imagine it gone — and reread what's left
after each removal. For each one, decide: does the remainder still
function as an apology, just a worse one, or does it stop functioning as
an apology at all? Note which single removal did the most damage to your
specific example.

Then trade with another pair. Compare notes: did the same component break
both apologies, or did a different one turn out to matter most in
theirs? If your two examples disagree about which component is
load-bearing, that disagreement is the finding, not a problem to resolve
— write down what was different about the two situations that made a
different component critical (who was harmed, how, and what they were
actually owed).

If removing every component seems to damage the apology by roughly the
same amount, resist the pressure to name a single winner anyway. That's
a real, reportable outcome — some apologies distribute the work evenly
across all four components rather than resting on one — and it's worth
noting which kind of harm produced that pattern, since Week 5 will ask
what an apology is actually for, and an apology with no single
load-bearing part may be one built to satisfy several functions at once.

Afterwards, hold onto both your ablation result and the comparison.
Later weeks will ask why a wronged person accepts a component-complete
apology and still doesn't feel repaired — that question starts from being
able to identify, case by case, which component that particular apology
was actually resting on.
