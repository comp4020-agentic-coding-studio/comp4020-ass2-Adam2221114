Before the session, have one apology ready — your own or a provided one —
short enough to deliver in a sentence or two.

In the session, produce two variants of it. For a timing pair: the same
wording delivered immediately, and the same wording delivered after a
delay you choose, where you're allowed to add exactly one sentence
showing what the delay was actually used for — no more. For a medium
pair: write out how the same wording plays as a text message and as a
transcript of it said face to face, noting stage directions (a pause, a
raised voice, a straight face) where the medium would show something text
can't.

For each pair, list what the recipient gains and what they lose: can
they reread it, ask something back right away, take their time replying,
or read something in how it was said that the words alone don't carry?
Then decide whether one version reads as more effective for your
specific case, and check that your reasoning points at timing or medium,
not a change you accidentally made to the wording itself.

If you can't tell whether a shift in how the apology lands came from the
timing or medium you changed, or from a wording difference you
introduced without meaning to, go back and diff your two versions word
for word before drawing any conclusion. This exercise only produces a
real finding if the wording actually held constant; a version that
quietly got more specific, or dropped a hedge, will look like a timing
or medium effect when it isn't one.

Afterwards, swap pairs with someone else. See whether they read the same
trade-off you did, or found a different capacity gained or lost that you
missed.
