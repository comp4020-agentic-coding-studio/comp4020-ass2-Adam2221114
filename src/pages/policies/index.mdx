## Using real people's apologies

This course works from real public apologies — you'll need at least one
for the Apology Autopsy, and you may draw on others across the semester.
"Public" means genuinely public: a press release, an interview, a public
social media post, a public response to a complaint. Not a private
message someone showed you in confidence, and not something you only
know because you happen to know the people involved. If you're unsure
whether a case counts as public, ask before you build an assessment
around it.

## You are never required to write about your own harm

Some sessions ask you to bring an apology you've personally sent or
received — Week 1, for instance. What you bring is entirely your choice.
Paraphrase instead of quoting if the exact wording would identify
someone. Use a case involving other people instead of your own if that's
what you'd rather hand in. No task in this course requires you to
disclose a personal conflict, and no marker needs to know which details
of a paraphrased example are real.

## Talking about the people in these cases

Analysing a public apology is not the same as diagnosing the person who
gave it. Keep the argument on the apology itself — its wording, its
timing, its audience — not on the character or private life of whoever
said it. If a case turns out to involve something beyond what you're
analysing (an unrelated scandal, someone's health, a legal matter still
underway), that's a reason to pick a different case, not extra material
for the assignment.

## AI and generative tools

You can use AI tools to help draft, edit, or organise your work. What you
can't outsource is the judgement this course actually teaches: which
component of an apology is doing the work, whether a pattern is a
non-apology, what a redesign should change and why. If a tool helped
produce any part of a submission, say so and say how — a line is
enough. Work submitted as your own diagnostic judgement should be your
own diagnostic judgement.

## Citing what you use

Quote or link the real apology you're analysing so a marker can check
your diagnosis against the source. An assessment built on a case you
can't produce evidence for isn't markable, however good the analysis
reads.
